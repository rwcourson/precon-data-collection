# Brasfield & Gorrie — Brand Messaging & Verbal Identity (2026)

The strategic half of the verbal identity, from the 2026 Brand Update (sections 2.1–2.9). `editorial-style.md` covers the *mechanics* of B&G prose (grammar, numbers, terminology); this file covers *what the brand says* — attributes, positioning, and the approved messaging frameworks for ads, proposals, community material, and recruitment.

## Governing rules (read these first)

1. **Headline + body copy are paired sets. Never mix and match** a headline from one set with body copy from another — the pairings are strategic, not interchangeable.
2. **Preserve capitalization and punctuation exactly as written.** Both are intentional parts of each message's tone. Exceptions only for graphic-design applications where typography/composition demand adaptation.
3. **Body copy may be shortened or refined** for format/space constraints — but any edit must keep the original tone, meaning, and the headline↔body relationship.
4. Advertising copy follows its own capitalization logic (below), distinct from editorial/long-form copy, which follows `editorial-style.md`.

## Capitalization in advertising copy
Grammatical structure determines the style:
- **Complete sentences** → sentence case, ending with punctuation.
- **Fragments and phrases** (typical of headlines and short-form copy) → **Title Case, no punctuation.**
- Exceptions where messaging needs sentence structure for impact.
- Content headers that act as a subject or label (not a complete thought) → Title Case.
- In Title Case, articles, prepositions, and conjunctions (a, the, of, in, with, and) stay lowercase unless they open the line.
- For long-form copy, use the editorial standard (`editorial-style.md`), not these rules.

## Brand attributes (2.1)
Five attributes define the voice and character. It's the combination that's ownable:

| Attribute | Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Grounded** | How we operate | Practical, steady, connected to the realities of the job. Decisions informed by experience, respect for people, and a clear understanding of what works. |
| **Tested** | Why we're trusted | Proven in the field. Decades of experience, hard lessons, and real-world challenges shape how we work. |
| **Deliberate** | How we execute | We act with purpose. Every decision — from early planning to final delivery — made in service of better outcomes and lasting value. |
| **Relentless** | How we respond to pressure | We pursue the right outcomes with focus and resolve. When challenges arise, we push forward, solve problems, and finish strong. |
| **Disciplined** | How we move forward | Clear standards and consistent execution — quality, safety, and accountability at every stage. |

## Positioning statement (2.2–2.3): "Building a Better Way"
The company-level headline, usable across audiences. Typeset at a consistent point size, **Title Case without punctuation** in most applications; **ALL CAPS allowed when used as a graphic**. It's a belief, not a boast — a commitment to always be in pursuit.

Pair it with the audience-appropriate body copy:

- **General brand awareness:** "For us, that means constantly seeking better, more meaningful ways to do the work. We approach each day as a chance to improve—never settling, never standing still, and always believing in the potential of big ideas and our ability to bring them to life."
- **Community-focused:** "We believe the strongest communities are built together—through compassion, opportunity, and people willing to invest in something bigger than themselves. That's why we're proud to support organizations making a lasting difference where we live and work."
- **Recruitment (students):** "The future of our industry depends on people ready to challenge what's possible. Here, you'll build more than projects—you'll build a career defined by growth, purpose, and the opportunity to shape what comes next."
- **Recruitment (industry):** "The people shaping the future of construction are the ones willing to keep pushing it forward. Here, your experience and ambition can lead to meaningful work, new opportunities for growth, and the chance to make a lasting impact on the industry."

## Using this file
- Building an ad, cover, banner, or campaign piece → pick ONE paired set matching the audience/purpose; typeset the headline per the advertising capitalization rules and the type system (`typography.md`).
- Writing a proposal or general prose → use the attributes and positioning as tonal guardrails; don't paste headlines mid-paragraph.
- If a needed message doesn't exist (new audience, new category), draft in the same voice and flag it as unapproved copy rather than presenting it as official messaging.
