# Brasfield & Gorrie — Editorial & Copy Style (Marketing Style Guide, Feb 2025)

The rules for how B&G *writes* — the verbal half of the brand. Apply to any prose representing the company: proposals, pursuits, captions, web copy, slide text, emails, project descriptions, social posts. The companion term/acronym lookups live in `references/terms-and-acronyms.md`; the conservative copy linter is `scripts/style_check.py`.

House baseline: AP-style-leaning with the B&G-specific overrides below. When writing for a client whose conventions differ, **adopt the client's style for consistency** (e.g., a client who prefers "MBE" over "MWBE").

## Company name & ampersand
- The name is **Brasfield & Gorrie** — always with the ampersand. **Never** substitute "and" for the "&".
- **Don't abbreviate it as "B&G"** — *except* when referring to **B&G Equipment and Supply** (the only sanctioned "B&G").
  - "BGES" should be **stylized as "B&G Equipment and Supply."** Internally: use "B&G Equipment and Supply" on first reference, then "BGES" or "the shop" thereafter. **Never** call it "the shop" in external communications.
- Refer to the company as **"our," not "its"** (e.g., "Brasfield & Gorrie holds performance as one of our core values").
- Whenever possible, **don't let "Brasfield & Gorrie" break across two lines** of text (use a non-breaking space).
- Company designations: **no comma before, and no periods within, "LLC"** (e.g., "Brasfield & Gorrie LLC"). Exception: legal writing using the registered name "Brasfield & Gorrie, L.L.C."
- **Ampersands** are only for company names (Brasfield & Gorrie). Don't use "&" for "and" anywhere else.

## Capitalization
- Don't capitalize **project, manager, architect, superintendent, owner** unless used as a title before a specific person's name. Capitalize a role only when it precedes the name (Project Manager Hank Williams); lowercase it when it follows (Betty White, project manager at Brasfield & Gorrie).
- **"general contractor"** is lowercase unless it follows a specific company name (S. Clemens, General Contractor LLC).
- Lowercase **federal, government, green** and seasons (spring, fall) unless part of a proper name (the Winter Olympics).
- **Capitalize "Black"** when referring to the racial group, as with other racial groups.
- Don't capitalize nouns like **hospital, government, laboratory, facility, central utility plant** unless part of a proper name (All Children's Hospital; UC Denver Central Utility Plant).

## Departments, divisions, regions, VDC
- **Department** names are capitalized including the word "Department" (Safety Department); lowercase "department" alone.
- **Division** names are capitalized including the word "Division" (Mission Critical Division); lowercase "division" alone.
- A group called a **"team"** is lowercase unless the name is a proper noun (Executive Team, COVID Response Team). Doesn't apply to brand names like Microsoft Teams. **"group"** is lowercase unless a proper team name (the East Group).
- **Market sectors** are lowercase in running text except where they precede "Division" (e.g., "the mission critical project was handled by the Mission Critical Division").
- Capitalize **"Region"** when it precedes a region number (Region 6); lowercase otherwise.
- **VDC:** the generic industry term is **"virtual design and construction" (lowercase) / "VDC."** Our department stylizes its name with a plus sign: **"Virtual Design + Construction" / "VDC Department."** Use the lowercase generic for the technology/process; use the department styling only for our specific department. (Note: some visual logo lockups render it "Virtual Design and Construction"; for written copy, follow this style-guide rule.)

## Numbers
- **Spell out under 10, numerals for 10 and above** for everything not a unit of measure (three windows; nine-story building; 85 floors; 15 lights).
- **Units of measure always use numerals** (2 feet; 9 inches; 4- to 6-foot beam; 2.8-acre block) **except measurements of time** (three hours; eight days).
- Cardinals: spell out up to 10, numerals after (three, eight, 22, 649). Ordinals: spell out first through ninth, figures for 10th+ (third, eighth, 22nd, 649th). Political/geographical order always uses figures (3rd District Court, 9th Ward).
- **No superscript ordinals** (22nd, not 22ⁿᵈ).
- If two numbers modify the same noun and one is ≥10, use numerals for both (8 to 10 buildings; 5- to 10-unit building).
- **Years are always numerals** regardless of position ("2024 was a banner year…"; "founded in 1964").
- **Phone numbers use periods:** 205.714.1280.
- Number before a noun in an adjectival phrase → hyphenate (85-unit complex; eight-story tower).
- **Ages** use numerals unless beginning a sentence ("Sixty-two-year-old Jim Gorrie").
- If a number **begins a sentence, spell it out** ("Forty-nine years ago, Miller Gorrie…").
- Prefer **"a" over the numeral "1"** where natural ("a cath lab," "a mile away").

## Time & dates
- Time of day: **10:30 a.m. / 10:30 p.m.** (lowercase, periods, space after the number). On the hour you may use 10:00 a.m. or 10 a.m. — be consistent. Spell out **noon** and **midnight** (not 12:00).
- Spell out **months**; for month + year, spell out the month (December 2009).
- **No "st/nd/rd/th"** after numbers in dates ("April 16 to 20," not "16th to 20th").
- **No apostrophe in decades** ("mid-1940s") unless decade-only ("the 1940s and '50s").
- Time zones: **CT, ET, MT, PT** — do **not** use CST/EST/MST/PST.

## Units of measurement
Abbreviations: cu ft, cu yd, **ft**, gal, **in.**, MW, mi, MGD, **lb** (sing./plural), psi, **sq ft**, sq mi, sq yd, ton, yd.
- **Space between number and unit** (5,000 psi; 49,332 sq ft).
- **Don't hyphenate** unit abbreviations even as a modifier (a 49,332 sq ft facility).
- Abbreviations are the **same singular and plural** (233 cu yd, not "cu yds").
- Use **"ft"** for linear feet (linearity is implied) unless spelling out is necessary.
- **Don't use "K"/"k"** for thousand (50,000 sq ft, not 50k).
- **Millions/billions:** one decimal for reading expediency ($1.4 million; 2.7 million sq ft); drop a trailing .0 ("$3 million," not "$3.0 million"). Don't use "Mil/mil" or "Bil/bil." If space forces abbreviation, capital "M"/"B" with a space ($628 M; $10 B).
- **Lumber:** "2 by 4" or "2 x 4" (not "2X4"/"2x4"); plural "2 by 4s" (no apostrophe); adjective "2-by-4 planks."
- **Ranges use the word "to,"** not a hyphen between values ("70 to 85% complete"; "10- to 15-foot span," not "10-15 foot").
- In running text, **don't use quotation marks for feet/inches** — spell out or use abbreviations (9-foot doors or 9 ft doors; 16 inches on center or 16 in. on center).

## Hyphens & dashes
- **No hyphen with "-ly" adverbs** ("a privately held general contractor").
- **Hyphenate compound adjectives before a noun** ("a well-known healthcare expert").
- **Prefixes generally take no hyphen** (preconstruction, multipurpose, nonprofit) unless needed for readability (multi-institutional).
- Hyphenate units of measurement as a compound modifier only when the unit is a whole word (a 27-foot ceiling; a 27 ft tree).
- In a **title/headline, capitalize all words in a hyphenated term** ("A Build-Out For the Future").
- **Hyphens (-)** join joint modifiers ("long-term savings," "low-rise building") and single concepts ("tie-in," "tilt-wall").
- **En dashes (–)** indicate ranges (pages 23–27) and replace hyphens in open compounds (Florida–Georgia border; pre–World War II era). They may separate a title/category from explanatory text, optionally with a space each side if applied consistently.
- **Em dashes (—)** replace commas for emphasis ("…the values we hold dear")—use sparingly. Dashes are generally closed (no surrounding spaces).
- After a dash or colon, capitalize the first following word only if it starts a complete sentence or is a proper noun (applies to colons too, not semicolons).

## Commas, lists, quotation marks, slashes, symbols
- **Oxford comma required** — never omit the final comma in a series.
- A city + state: **both the city and the state are followed by a comma** ("The 49,322 sq ft project in Apopka, Florida, is…").
- **Comma after an introductory phrase** ("Before the project begins, the team…").
- Titles of **numbered publications** are italicized and set off by commas ("As stipulated in UFC 3-310-04, Seismic Design for Buildings, inspections…").
- **Lists:** capitalize the first word of each item; no end periods unless items are complete sentences or the last item completes the introductory sentence; if one item needs a period, all do. Keep bullets brief. Use a comma before **"including"** when it precedes a list.
- **Quotation marks:** use sparingly; not around commonly understood words. Commas, periods, and exclamation points go **inside**; colons and semicolons **outside**. Question marks go inside if the quoted part is the question, outside if the whole sentence is the question.
- **Slashes:** preferred style is no space on either side (one space each side only if readability demands, applied consistently). Avoid using slashes to signify alternatives.
- **Symbols:** use the **% symbol** (not the word "percent") in running text for accessibility and space. One space after a period — never two.

## Acronyms & abbreviations
- Define on **first use** with the acronym in parentheses, then use the acronym ("…building information modeling (BIM)… BIM offers…"). No definition needed for industry-standard common acronyms (HVAC).
- If a term appears **only once**, don't acronym it unless industry-standard and common.
- **Plurals add "s," no apostrophe** (JSAs, MRIs).
- "a" vs "an" before an acronym follows the **sound** ("a VIP," "an MBA").
- **No shorthand abbreviations** — write "superintendents" not "sups," "specifications" not "specs," "modified bitumen" not "mod bit."
- **No comma before "Inc."** in company names ("Good Builders Inc.").
- See `terms-and-acronyms.md` for the "do not spell out" list and the full common-acronyms list with B&G-specific stylizations.

## LEED & registered marks
- **LEED®** is a registered trademark: place the ® after the first instance of LEED **on every page**. (Same ® rule for **LPS®**, Last Planner System.) **LEED AP is not trademarked.**
- **Buildings are "LEED certified"; people are "LEED accredited."**
- "certified" is included but lowercase in a certification ("LEED Gold certified"); hyphenate as an adjective ("a LEED-certified project").
- Don't spell out **LEED AP** as "LEED Accredited Professional." Spell out **LEED Green Associate** (not "LEED GA").
- **LEED-certified confidential projects** must not reference their LEED certification.

## Preferred terminology
- "building envelope" — not "building skin"
- "build-out" — not "up-fit" or "fit-up"
- "prefabricated" primarily; "prefab" only sparingly to avoid repetition
- **"value analysis" — not "value engineering"**
- "project" (not "job") for the whole project — but don't overuse after project names
- "subcontractor" primarily; "trade contractor" only sparingly
- **"honored," not "proud,"** when referring to the company's own merits/successes. "proud" is fine for supporting clients/partners/organizations ("proud to support the AHA Heart Ball").

## Cities, states & regions
- **Don't abbreviate state names in running text;** abbreviations are OK in gutters/callouts where space is tight (use USPS: AL, FL, GA).
- If a city or state is part of a **project name**, don't repeat it; do include the location that isn't part of the name (Clemson Memorial Stadium in South Carolina; The University of Florida in Gainesville).
- Capitalize a **cardinal direction** when it names a specific region ("the largest general contractor in the Southeast") but lowercase when merely descriptive ("our office is in southwest Birmingham").

## Writing & editing tips
- One space after a period. Reduce "in order to" → "to"; "all of" → "all". Avoid "utilize" unless it means "to use to best effect."
- Avoid wordy phrases ("due to the fact that" → "because"; "that being the case" → "consequently"). Avoid overusing "very" (try significantly, extremely, particularly, thoroughly, substantially, immensely).
- Use **"forward/backward"** (not "forwards/backwards"). Use **"ensure"** (+ "that") for making certain; "insure" only for insurance.
- **"amount"** for mass nouns (amount of water); **"number"** for count nouns (number of workers).
- Place **"only"** after the verb, not between subject and verb.
- **"farther"** for physical distance; **"further"** for time/degree.
- Revise sentences starting with "there is/are" or "it is" into direct, active form ("We can mitigate risk in various ways").
- Non-dialogue quotes (e.g., a mission statement) use **"says"** (present); dialogue uses **"said"** (past).
- Fix dangling/misplaced modifiers. Pluralize the most important word in multiword terms (attorneys general; land ports of entry).
- **Completed projects → past tense; the structures themselves → present tense** ("The Protective Stadium project was a challenging build"; "Protective Stadium is a great place to see a game").
- **"which"** (with a comma) for nonessential clauses; **"that"** for essential clauses.
- Avoid redundancy ("each and every" → "each"/"every"; drop needless "any"/"existing"). Avoid jargon ("preplanning"/"advance planning" → "planning"; "in real time" → "immediate").
- Avoid "intimate" for knowledge/experience — use "extensive," "thorough," "in-depth."
- **"effect"** = noun (a result); **"affect"** = verb (to influence).
- When hyperlinking, don't include end punctuation or dashes in the linked text.
